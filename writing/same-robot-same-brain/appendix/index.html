Appendix · Systems note 001

The plates behind the note Three full-size drawings of what a swap does to the session.

Each plate shows the same run three ways: where the duck went, how fast it went, and the resident session underneath, which never restarted. They are the evidence behind the figures in Change a walking robot's software without stopping it.

Shinro · 24 October 2026 · Appendix · Cake

Contents

  1. The navigator swap
  2. Standing to walking
  3. One walking brain for another

Every swap is one Cake update transaction, and every plate draws its five stages along the bottom: the dependency closure is computed, the retiring generation is drained, the new binding is applied to the live snapshot, the snapshot is published, and the old generation's memory is reclaimed. What the plates are for is the line above those stages: the session identity, and the parts that keep running while the transaction does its work.

A1The navigator swap

The duck walks the circle, then the figure 8, then the slalom, and the navigator is replaced under it at 8 and 16 seconds. The path bends at each swap and never breaks. Each new navigator starts with the cross-track error the old one left behind — 0.23 m for the figure 8, 0.37 m for the slalom — and drives it back to zero within a few seconds. There is no pause to hand over, so there is nothing to hide in the error line.

A2Standing to walking

The same transaction, aimed at the brain instead of the navigator. The duck balances under the standing network for the first two and a half seconds, the swap lands, and by the end of the ten-second run it is walking. Two things are deliberately outside the swap: the bridge, which owns the simulator link and the 50 Hz clock, and the navigator, which is still the circle and does not notice.

A3One walking brain for another

The like-for-like case, and the one in Demo A of the note: walk to walk_velstand, same duck, same request. Only the network that is driving changes. The bridge, the clock and the circle all carry on, which is what makes the swap boring from the software's point of view — and the reason the interesting question becomes what the incoming network does to the duck.

These plates are single runs. The run-by-run tables, including the swap direction that does not work, are in the note.
