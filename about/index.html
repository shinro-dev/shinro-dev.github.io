About

The workbench
and the notes.

Shinro builds the layer between a trained policy and a moving machine: compile it, admit it, replace it, and show what happened. This site is the public notebook; the product is at shinro.dev.

01 — What we make

Three surfaces, one stack.

The product, the open framework and the demos are different views of the same layer: what runs the policy, what admits it, and what happens when it has to change.

01 · THE PRODUCT

Studio and the cake kernel

Describe the machine, compose behavior from visual modules, simulate it, deploy a native binary, and swap modules on a running robot. Studio is source-available; the kernel is commercial.

shinro.dev ↗
02 · THE OPEN FRAMEWORK

shinro-python-modules

Five abstractions — controller, plant, estimator, trajectory, physics — composed from TOML, traced, and lowered to a Zig kernel for the target. This is where the compiler and the control vocabulary live.

Shinro-xyz/shinro-python-modules ↗
03 · THE DEMOS

Microduck and LeKiwi

A simulated biped that swaps its policy mid-stride, and a bench robot that names the session before it moves. Two demos, two claims, one kernel.

See the demos ↗

02 — Two organizations

Why the work is split in two.

The split is practical, not organisational: the framework and the simulation demos keep the compiler and the control abstractions together; this site and the four-click demo keep the operator contract and the evidence together.

03 — How we publish

Claims with sources.

The notes are evidence-first: every claim is tied to a recording, a run record or a source file, and the limits are stated next to the claims. When we get something wrong, we would rather correct it in public than leave it standing.

Demos are source-available for personal, educational and non-commercial use; commercial deployment requires a licence from Shinro SAS. New notes are on RSS; corrections go to the issues of the demo repository in question.

Talk to us.

If you are building a robot and the control layer is the part you keep rebuilding, we would like to hear about it.
